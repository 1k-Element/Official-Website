How to read this

Temp shows air temperature. Air carries its heat wherever it flows, so you can watch cool air spill out of an aircon room or warm outdoor air push in through a window.

Feels like subtracts the cooling effect of moving air on skin, including the downdraft under a ceiling fan. Comfortable here means feeling 23–27 °C.

Heat from the ceiling models a sun-baked roof. The ceiling becomes a warm surface that slowly heats the air beneath it and radiates onto people, so "feels like" rises even where the air is cool. It applies to every enclosed room; choose None for rooms with another floor above.

Aircons behave like inverter units: each reads the air at the unit and throttles down as the room nears its setpoint. Capacity sets how much air it moves.

Speed finds acceleration through doorways and around corners. Pressure shows windward push warm and leeward suction cool; cross ventilation works when an opening sits in each. Swirl shows air trapped recirculating.

Side view cuts the house from roof to ground. Here warm air rises and cool air sinks, so you can watch heat pool under the roof, warmth climb the stairwell, and cold air from an aircon fall to the floor. The steel roof sits at the temperature you set. Plywood stops air but lets heat seep through, and the hot roof also radiates onto whatever is beneath it. Erase part of a wall to open a window, part of the ceiling to join a room to the roof space, or part of the roof to vent it.

The floor plan is a flat slice at about standing height, where rising warm air can't be shown, so there heat rides along with the air without pushing it. Both views are 2D slices. In the side view the lift of warm air is strengthened so that it shows at this scale, so read draught speeds there as a guide only. Walls leak a little heat to and from outdoors but don't store it, and sun through windows isn't modelled. Because air can't escape over the roof in 2D, wind pushes through a house harder than in reality; treat indoor breeze speeds as an upper bound. Use it to compare options, not to size equipment.
